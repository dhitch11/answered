The moment they answer

The wait is the cheap part.

Anybody can hold. What matters is the ten seconds after a human says hello.

01

It says who it is

First sentence, every time: an AI assistant calling for you, with your permission. Nobody is tricked, so nobody hangs up.

02

It states your case

Your account, your dates, your amounts. It does not get flustered, tired, or talked in circles.

03

It rings you in

You are on the call in about four seconds, agent already briefed. You skipped the queue, not the conversation.

04

It writes it down

Who you spoke to, what they promised, and when. The next call starts where this one ended.

If the line drops or the queue resets you, it starts over. Redials are not a second charge. Neither is the second hour.

Where the line is

It makes your case. It will not promise you an answer that belongs to somebody else to give.

It says it is an AI, to the human it reaches.

First sentence, every call. Several states now require it. We would do it anyway: an agent that hides what it is loses the call.

You authorize each call. It does not go dialing on its own.

One you asked for, to a number you named, about a matter you described. No autodialer, no campaign, no list.

You will always know the price before it dials.

Any share of a credit that lands will be capped, disclosed before the call, and published only with a substantiation file behind it.

What it costs

You pay for a person, not for the waiting.

Two hours of hold music costs you nothing. The charge exists only when a human is on the line.
