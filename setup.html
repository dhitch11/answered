Step two

Set the rules before it says a word.

No call flow builder. No tree with forty branches. Eight things, in your words.

01 Name and voice

What it is called and how it sounds.

  • It answers as your shop, not as us.
  • Temperament: unflappable, cheerful, or politely relentless.
  • It says it is an AI in the first sentence, every time.

02 Hours

When you are open, and what after hours means.

  • Standard: 7:00a to 5:00p, Monday to Friday.
  • After hours: emergencies only.
  • Everything else gets a slot in the morning, not a voicemail.

03 What it may book

And what it must never book.

  • Books: service calls, estimates, repeat customers.
  • Never books: mobile homes, commercial roofs, anything you do not do.
  • It says no cleanly instead of booking a job you cancel Monday.

04 Service area

Where you go, and what happens past the line.

  • Drawn by zip, not by a circle on a map.
  • Past Route 9: it says the $95 trip charge before it books.
  • Outside the area: it says so and offers to take a message.

05 Emergency

What counts, and where it goes.

  • Your words: water in the house, gas smell, no heat under 40.
  • It calls your cell with a nine second brief before you say hello.
  • No answer in twenty seconds: your second, then your third, then it books the soonest slot and keeps the customer.

06 What it must never say

Prices, unless you say otherwise.

  • The default is no number, ever.
  • It gives your range or it hands the caller to a person.
  • It does not guess, round, or estimate on your behalf.

07 A complaint, or a hot caller

It does not argue with your customer.

  • Warranty: it says the word out loud and flags the ticket.
  • Angry: it stays level, takes the detail, books the callback.
  • You get texted first, before the callback is confirmed.

08 Where the job lands

Your calendar, your phone, or paper.

  • Google Calendar, Outlook, a shared team calendar.
  • Or just a text, with the day's list at 6.
  • Running on paper is a real answer and it does not argue with you.

Every rule is one sentence in your own words, and it stays in your words. You can read your whole setup out loud. Most shops fit on one page.

    Step four

    Try to break it.

    Call it drunk on a Friday. Talk over it. Ask what it costs. Be rude. We would rather you did it than a customer.

    You talk over it.

    It stops talking and listens. It does not restart the sentence you already heard.

    You ask what it costs.

    It refuses to invent a number. You get your range, or a person.

    You get rude.

    It stays level, keeps taking the detail, and never gets rude back.

    You ramble at midnight.

    It pulls the four things it needs and lets the rest go by.

    You ask if it is a person.

    It already told you it is not. It tells you again, plainly.

    You describe a real emergency.

    It stops booking and calls your cell, briefed, in nine seconds.
